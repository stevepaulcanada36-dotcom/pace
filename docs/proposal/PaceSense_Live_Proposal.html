Starting point

What the paper showed, and what it could not see

Buy-in went up

Sliders raised acceptance (β = 0.266, p < .001) and perceived competence (β = 0.780, p < .001).

Coaches improved the model

Adapted predictions were closer to the held-out best race: 3.14% → 2.33% mean error (p = .018).

But steering was invisible

Some coaches dragged the model onto their own estimate, then “accepted” it. Same score as being persuaded.

“When a coach moves a slider, is that expertise correcting the model, or the model being steered toward what the coach already believed?”
The open question from the paper's discussion (Sec. 5.7 and 6)
Muijlwijk et al., IUI '242
The gap

Four things a slider cannot do

1 · It is silent

A slider at 0.2 stores a number, never the reason. The study cannot tell “hot day, injured” from “I just think she's faster”.

2 · Coaches think in stories

In the think-aloud sessions coaches talked: “Here she hit the wall, maybe it was hot weather” (P7). The interface keeps none of it.

3 · Nothing checks the change

The coach sees the new finish time, but not whether the data supports lowering that race or quietly contradicts it.

4 · Steering is found too late

Model Adaptation was computed afterwards, for researchers. The coach never sees that the model is drifting onto their own estimate.

Quotes: think-aloud sessions, Sec. 5.23
Proposal

The coach talks. The sliders follow. The data checks.

🎙️

Speak

Continuous listening. “2014 was a hot day and she was injured” moves the 2014 slider down, and keeps those words as the reason.

📊

Check

Each move is compared with the runner's other races: data agrees your knowledge against the data

🪞

Reflect

A live steering meter: the paper's Model Adaptation, computed while the coach works. At 1.0 the model has become the coach's own estimate.

Not a chatbot that predicts: the same case-based model as the paper. Voice only changes how the coach steers it, and makes the steering visible.

Statistics

Four measures behind every move

Race evidence

zi = (ti − med(t−i)) / max(1.4826·MAD(t−i), 0.02·med)

Is this race unusual compared with the runner's other races? Robust to one bad race; |z| ≥ 1 counts as unusual.

Evidence check

Direction of the change vs. sign of z:

data agrees lower a slow race · your knowledge race is typical · against the data lower a fast race

Influence

pulli = wi(pi − F) / Σw, Σ pull = 0

Seconds each race drags the prediction faster or slower; plus the exact change if it were left out.

Steering index (live)

S = (Fnow − Fequal) / (Coachestimate − Fequal)

The paper's Model Adaptation (Sec. 5.7), shown while the coach works. 0 = the data's view, 1 = the coach's own.

Data-driven defaults

“Trust the recent races more”: how much more?

From the paper

Coaches made newer races more representative (slider ~ year, β = 0.042, p < .001).

Fitted, not guessed

w = 2−years before latest / h. h is chosen by the paper's hold-out protocol on a seeded sample.

Honest caveat

Synthetic data with improvement built in: this shows the method works, not that real runners behave this way.

Evaluation plan

A third arm on the paper's own design

ArmCoach can
A · Non-interactivesee the equal-weights prediction (paper)
B · Slidersdrag one slider per race (paper)
C · PaceSense Livetalk; sliders follow; evidence labels and steering meter

× familiar / unfamiliar runners (within coach), 4 runners each, as in the paper. Unfamiliar runners keep the held-out best race for accuracy.

Measures

Paper: acceptance, perceived competence, willingness to depend, accuracy, Model Adaptation.
New: share of steered decisions (adaptation ≥ 0.85), share of changes with a reason, evidence-consistent changes, SUS and NASA-TLX.

H1 · Acceptance

C is not lower than B (non-inferiority, margin 0.10).

H2 · Less steering (primary)

C has fewer steered decisions than B.

H3 · Accuracy

C's adapted error ≤ B's (paper: 2.33%).

H4 · Familiarity

The steering reduction is larger for familiar runners (paper RQ2/RQ3).

Analysis as the paper: multilevel models, random intercept per coach (pacesense.analysis)10

Limits

Risks, and what the design does about them

RiskMitigation
Privacy of speechBrowser speech (Chrome, Edge) sends audio to a cloud service. Local Whisper mode keeps audio on the laptop; the study would use it, with recording consent.
Misrecognition (accents, noise)Every move shows the words it came from; “scratch that” undoes; unclear speech changes nothing and says so.
Deference to the labelsA typical race is labelled your knowledge, not unsupported: an injury never shows in a finish time.
The meter suppresses expertiseExactly what H1 (acceptance) and H3 (accuracy) test; the meter can be hidden.
AI reader hallucinatesIt only proposes; code drops unknown races and reasons that were not said.
Synthetic dataAll numbers here are from synthetic runners; the study uses real coaches and real race results.
