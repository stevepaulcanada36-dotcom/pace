# The paper's analysis in PaceSense

How the measures in Muijlwijk et al. (IUI '24), as presented in the course presentation, appear in the app.
The **Your session** tab (or saying "how did I do?") shows them for the session on screen.

| Paper | In PaceSense |
| --- | --- |
| Acceptance (weight of advice) and Model adaptation; the four cases: coach persuaded, met halfway, model steered to the coach, steered beyond | **Whose view**: the coach's first and latest estimates against the model's equal-weight and current targets, drawn on one time line, with the case named. Cases 3 and 4 score full acceptance without persuasion, and the app says so. |
| RQ4: coaches' slider settings cut the error against the held-out best race (3.14% to 2.33%) | **Accuracy against the hidden race**: after "reveal the real result", % off for equal weights, the coach's model and the coach's own estimates. |
| Slider position rose with race year (beta = 0.042) | **Recent races**: the slope of the coach's weights against race year, compared with the paper's. |
| Knowledge coaches used: pacing consistency, mental strength, training load, injuries, untapped potential, personal circumstances | **Knowledge you used**: what the coach said, grouped into those categories (plus race-day conditions). |
| RQ2: less acceptance for familiar runners | **Familiarity**: what knowing the runner means here; the Bias guard and Data check loops challenge more for known runners. |
| "Whose beliefs win?": expertise or confirmation bias | **Expertise or expectation?**: how many changes went against the runner's own times; the Bias guard asks when the model nears the coach's own estimate. |
| Case-based reasoning: the prediction reuses what similar runners went on to run | Each change card's **Why**: the target is the weighted average of what similar runners ran after each race, so a race counted less moves the target away from that race's own prediction. |

All numbers come from the model and the session; none are generated by a language model.

## Neither the AI's bias nor the coach's

The paper's main caution is that interaction can let a coach steer the model to what they already expected and
still score full Acceptance. PaceSense treats itself as a fair second view: the coach decides, and PaceSense
makes the trade-off visible.

- **Weigh it**, on every change card: the case for (the data agrees, the coach gave a concrete reason), the case
  against (it goes against the runner's own times, it was said as a hope rather than a fact, it makes the
  target rest on one race, it is smaller than the model's uncertainty), and what would settle it.
- **One spoken counterpoint** after a change, when there is one.
- **Second opinion** ("what's the case against my changes?", "is the target overfitting?", "am I biased?"): a
  verdict and the points on each side. With a hosted model it is a structured analysis of a fact sheet written
  by PaceSense, and any sentence quoting a number not on that sheet is dropped; without one, or if nothing
  survives, it is built from the same numbers by rule.
- **The assistant** is told not to agree because the coach insists, to separate knowledge from hope, to give the
  strongest point on the other side of any judgement, and to name the limits below when they matter.

Limits stated to the coach: the model sees only past marathon times; the target is a good-day goal; full
agreement can come from steering rather than persuasion; familiarity changes reliance; one revealed race checks a
change but does not prove a method; the study was small and these runners are synthetic.
